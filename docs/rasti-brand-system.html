Rasti

The visual system for the match database — results, forecasts and calibration across the Premier League, Championship and League One. Built to be read on a phone, in bad light, quickly.

The rule everything else follows

Colour means outcome, not success

Most football sites colour a result green when your team won and red when it lost. That's wrong for this database. A ledger has no team. 1, X and 2 are three categories of equal standing, so they get three colours of equal weight — blue, graphite, brick.

Whether a forecast hit is a separate question, and it never uses colour. It uses form: a filled underline on the mark, and a ✓ or ✕ in ink. This keeps a losing prediction on a home win looking exactly as calm as a winning one, which is the whole point of keeping score honestly.

02 — Type

Three faces, one hard rule

Montserrat for display, Roboto for reading, Roboto Mono for everything countable. Carried over from the Storm briefings so the research reports and the site read as one body of work.

The hard rule: every number on this site is Roboto Mono with tabular figures. Scores, probabilities, RPS values, matchweek numbers, dates. It is what lets a column of results scan straight down without the eye stumbling. No exceptions, including inside prose.

04 — Layout

Phone first, 720px ceiling

A single column, 720px maximum, 16px gutters. This is a reading-and-scanning site, not a dashboard — widening it to fill a desktop monitor would only stretch the match rows apart. On a large screen the column simply centres with more air around it.

Spacing runs on a 4px base. Row padding is 12px 16px; sections are separated by 48px and a hairline.

4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64

Navigation model

League → matchweek → match. Three levels, no more. The top bar carries the wordmark and the theme toggle only; league switching sits in the page, not in a hamburger menu, because there are only three leagues and they should all be one tap away.

05 — Rules

Non-negotiables

  • Colour encodes outcome only. Never green-for-good or red-for-bad. A missed forecast is shown with form, not hue.
  • All numerals are Roboto Mono, tabular. Including inside body text.
  • Probability triples always read 1 / X / 2, in that order, everywhere — bars, legends, tables, prose.
  • Unforecast matches are shown, not hidden, with an empty coupon and a "No forecast" tag. The record has to include what we didn't call.
  • Never backfill a prediction. A row without a forecast at kickoff stays without one forever.
  • Pending fixtures use a dashed tag, never a placeholder score or a zero.
  • Coupon squares keep square corners. Everything else uses the radius scale.
  • Don't add a colour for each league, a gradient, or a team crest. Crests would import twenty other colour systems into this one.
  • Tap targets ≥ 44px. Match rows are tappable in full, not just the team name.
  • Theme is remembered, and respects the system setting on first visit.
06 — Voice

Two voices, and a wall between them

The interface is flat. Labels label, stat cells state a number, empty states say what to do. "No forecast made" — never "N/A", which reads as missing data rather than a deliberate absence. No hype, no exclamation marks, no personality in the chrome.

The commentary is the opposite: opinionated, sharp, brutal about bad decisions, generous about genuine excellence, and funny when a match earns it. Match reports that read like a wire service are worse than no commentary at all.

The wall

Opinion lives in content/**/*.md and nowhere else. It never touches a forecast probability, a mark, an RPS figure, or anything on the calibration page. A forecast is a claim about the world made before kickoff; if the numbers start bending toward the team you want to win, everything computed downstream becomes decoration. The commentary is where the opinions go precisely so the forecasts can stay clean.

Declared bias

Arsenal is the favourite team; Ipswich Town gets a soft spot. Stated openly, because a reader who works it out themselves will assume it was hidden everywhere else. Both are held to the same standard as everyone else, and the excuses get cut — the tell of a homer isn't praise, it's mitigation.

✓ Commentary

  • "West Ham spent twenty minutes after the second goal playing like men who'd just been handed a leaflet explaining that the Championship is a real league."
  • "QPR 0–0 Bolton was ninety minutes of two teams politely declining to attack each other, and it went in the coupon as a single. That's on me."

⚠ Interface

  • Stat cell: 4/8 · Correct
  • Empty state: "No matches logged for this week yet."
  • Pending: "Kicks off Sunday 14:00."

✕ Neither

  • Shouting without a specific — "ABSOLUTE CARNAGE", "DISGRACEFUL"
  • Automated filler — "a disappointing result for the visitors"
  • Speculation about anyone's private life, health or character
  • Punching down at fans, cities or accents
  • Excuses for Arsenal or Ipswich
