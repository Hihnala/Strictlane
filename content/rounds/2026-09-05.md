---
title: Post-round review
summary: The first round to finish ahead of the closing market, mostly on one match that moved after the forecast was written.
---
The best round so far on hits, and the first one where the forecast finished
ahead of the closing market. The second deserves less credit than it sounds.

**The margin is Millwall.** The forecast went in on Thursday with Millwall clear
favourites at home to Bolton. By kickoff the market had gone right off them.
Millwall won 4–0. That one match is worth more than the whole round's margin;
take it out and the other twelve lose to the close. I didn't know something the
market didn't. The market learned something, or thought it did, after I'd
written the number down, and the result ignored it.

**The mirror image is West Brom.** The price moved towards them between Thursday
and kickoff, they beat Watford 1–0, and it was the costliest leg on the coupon.
Of the five matches that moved furthest after capture, the result followed the
move three times. The biggest of the five was Millwall, and it didn't. That, not
insight, is the margin.

**What I got wrong.** Burnley at home to Bristol City went in as a single on a
side that hadn't won in four and then produced eight shots and 0.26 xG. That was
reputation doing the forecasting. Brentford against Sunderland was the other
single to fail, and the draw was fair: Sunderland had five shots on target to
two. Forest against Spurs was the most evenly priced match on the card and I left
the draw off it.

**Draws.** Two landed unmarked, both in the Premier League, and neither in the
kind of balanced Championship fixture the log has been flagging. The pattern
didn't recur. Under 8+0 the draw is excluded from most of the coupon, so losing
two or three a round is arithmetic, not a bias to be corrected. Keep measuring
it; stop lamenting it.

**The adjustments.** The West Ham trim cost again, against a Derby side that
didn't register a shot on target, and has been retired. The Hull nudge finished
a hair better than the close: one data point, not a rule. The balanced-fixture
draw raise paid on Forest and cost on Sheffield United, and stays unproven.

**Correction to the record.** This week the market benchmark was rebuilt from
football-data's closing average for every match this season, replacing a mix of
midweek and matchday prices. 22 August, previously a narrow forecast win, is now
a narrow market win. 29 August is still a market win, for different reasons than
its review first gave; that review has been rewritten. The season still belongs
to the market.

**Note for the model.** The forecast is written on Thursday; the benchmark is
Saturday's close. Every round, the market gets two days of team news the forecast
doesn't, and two rounds running the biggest single legs were matches that moved
after capture. Worth deciding before the next coupon whether to write later or to keep Thursday and accept that some of
every round's result is timing rather than judgement.
