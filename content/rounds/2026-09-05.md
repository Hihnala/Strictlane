Ten from thirteen, the best round of the season so far. The hedges did the
work. The singles mostly didn't.

Seven of the eight hedges landed. The wildest of them was at Craven Cottage,
where Fulham led Crystal Palace 2–1 at half-time and lost 3–2. Both winners
were on the coupon, so the line survived a collapse it had no business
surviving. The same split covered Norwich winning 3–1 at Sheffield United and
Portsmouth beating Cardiff 2–0. Brighton and Hull were both hedged to include
the draw, and both drew: Brighton came back from a goal down at the break to
draw 1–1 with Leeds, and Hull and Aston Villa managed 0–0.

The one hedge that missed was Nottingham Forest against Tottenham. A tight
fixture, the draw left out, and a 0–0 to show for it.

Three of the five singles landed. Manchester City beat Coventry 1–0, West Ham
beat Derby 3–0 and Millwall put four past Bolton without reply. The other two
went wrong in the second half. Brentford were the pool's clear favourites at
home to Sunderland, it was 0–0 at the break, and it finished 1–1. Burnley
were level with Bristol City at half-time and lost 2–1 at home. Burnley
hadn't won in four, and backing them alone was reputation doing the
forecasting.

Four draws, two of them covered. Both misses came in the Premier League, not
in the balanced Championship fixtures earlier notes had been flagging, so
that pattern didn't show up this week. Under 8+0 the draw is left out of most
of the coupon, and losing a couple of them a round is the cost of the system
rather than a bias to correct. Keep measuring it. Stop lamenting it.

Note for the model. The forecast went in on Thursday, which means it worked
from Thursday's team news, not Saturday's. Either write it later or accept
that part of every round's result is timing rather than judgement.
