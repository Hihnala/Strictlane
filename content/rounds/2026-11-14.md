Ten from thirteen, level with the season's best for the second week running.
It's the season's fourth ten, after 5 September, 17 October and 7 November. The
whole coupon was League One and League Two, with eight of the thirteen from a
division I don't follow. It didn't show in the count.

The singles went four from five, the season's best alongside 31 October.
Stockport beat Reading 3–1, MK Dons beat Burton 6–2, Port Vale beat Accrington
4–2 and York won 1–0 at Rochdale. Every single sided with the pool's
favourite, which makes three weeks without one against the crowd.

Salford was the miss. The clearest favourite on the coupon, at home, with the
biggest pool majority of the round. They trailed Northampton at half-time and
drew 1–1. That's ordinary variance. Salford alone was the right single and I'd
play it again.

**What I got wrong.** The draw, again. This coupon carried it seven times, the
most this season, and both draws landed in matches without it. Crewe were 0–2
down to Newport at the break and drew 2–2 in a hedge that kept home and away.
Salford was the other. That's eleven draws in five rounds and not one covered.

The costliest call was AFC Wimbledon. The hedge was home and away until
Saturday morning, when I switched it to the draw and the away win to follow
the open-match rule from 31 October. Wimbledon beat Blackpool 1–0. The pool had
the two sides level, so there was no crowd read to lean on either way. The rule
cost a hit here. The first version would have made it eleven.

The same rule put the draw in at Sheffield Wednesday, Wigan, Shrewsbury and
Bristol Rovers. None of them finished level. Five open matches, no draws and
one hit lost.

Grimsby won 4–1 at Swindon and Chesterfield won 2–0 at Bristol Rovers. Both
were pool favourites on the road and both hedges left out the home win. Eight
home wins, two draws and three away wins. 44 goals, the most of any round this
season.

Note for the model. Keep the open-match rule for now. One round where it cost
a hit and found no draws isn't enough to drop a rule built on two months of
coupons. Log it all the same: five uses, no draws, one hit lost. If the next
two rounds look like this one, revisit how close home and away have to be.
The draws keep turning up in matches the coupon reads as one-sided, Crewe and
Salford this time. No single went against the pool for the third week running,
so the 10 October rule is still untested.
