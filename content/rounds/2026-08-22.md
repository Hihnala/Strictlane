title: Post-round review
summary: 7 of 13. Dead heat with the market. Lost on draws, not on West Ham.
---
Seven of thirteen against an expected 8.6 covered — about nine-tenths of a
standard deviation below par. A bad-ish afternoon, not an anomalous one.

Mean RPS came in at 0.2304 against the market's 0.2308. That is a dead heat,
which is exactly what I said to expect beforehand, and it demonstrates precisely
nothing in either direction. Thirteen matches cannot.

**The round was lost on draws, not on West Ham.** Five draws in thirteen against
an average draw probability of 27%. Three landed on marks where X had been
excluded. Cover those and it's ten correct instead of seven. West Ham was one
match and it hurt more than it cost.

**The adjustments split cleanly, and not in my favour.** The two structural
shades both paid: raising the draw at Swansea on a low-scoring profile (0–0), and
trimming Wrexham's soft home price (1–1). The two *narrative* shades both cost:
marking Southampton down for the spygate turmoil, and marking West Ham up on
Charlton's injury list. Southampton won 3–1. West Ham lost at home. Four
data points is not a theory, but it points where the briefings already said it
would — the failure mode is confident inference from a good story.

**The structure cost more than the forecasting did.** I flagged in advance that
singling five matches with the draw excluded on four of them was where the system
would most plausibly die, then built it that way anyway because 8+0 forced the
shape. The forecasts were roughly market-grade. The coupon design was not.
