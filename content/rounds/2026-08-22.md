---
title: Round review — 22 August 2026
summary: Seven from thirteen on the opening round. Five draws, and three of them landed on matches where the draw had been left out.
---
Seven from thirteen to open the season. A bad afternoon, not a freak one.

The round was lost on draws. There were five of them, and three fell on
matches where the draw wasn't on the coupon. Derby 2–2 Cardiff and QPR 0–0
Bolton were both home singles. Wycombe 1–1 Plymouth was hedged on either side
winning and got neither. Cover those three and it's ten correct. The two draws
that were covered, Swansea 0–0 Sheffield United and Wrexham 1–1 Watford, both
landed.

West Ham 1–2 Charlton was the loud miss. Charlton were ahead at the London
Stadium by half-time and won with six first-teamers missing, against a West
Ham side played as a single. It hurt more than anything else on the card. The
draws cost more.

Five of the eight hedges came in. Brentford were 2–0 up on Tottenham at the
break and won 3–0, Everton beat Crystal Palace 2–0, and Ipswich opened the
season by beating Sunderland 2–1 at home. Apart from Wycombe, the misses were
Forest, beaten 1–0 at home by Leeds, and Blackburn, who were 1–0 down to
Middlesbrough at half-time and won 2–1 with the home win ruled out.

The four deliberate adjustments split cleanly, and not in my favour. The two
built on how teams play both paid: the draw at Swansea was raised on a
low-scoring profile and it finished 0–0, and Wrexham's soft home price was
trimmed before a 1–1. The two built on a story both cost. Southampton were
marked down over the spygate fallout and beat Stoke 3–1. West Ham were marked
up on Charlton's injury list and lost at home. Four data points isn't a
theory, but it points where you'd guess: the failure mode is a confident
inference from a good story.

Note for the model. The draw was left out of eight matches on a
thirteen-match coupon. I knew beforehand that this was where the coupon was
most likely to die and built it that way anyway, because 8+0 forced the shape.
The forecasts held up on their own terms. The coupon design didn't.
