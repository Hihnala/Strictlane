Ten from thirteen, level with the season's best (5 September and 17 October).
It's the first round this season in which all eight hedges held. The singles
went two from five, so eight of the ten came from the cover.

Seven hedges kept home and away and left out the draw. The eighth was Crewe
against AFC Wimbledon, with the home win and the draw covered and the away win
left out. Crewe won 2–1. The hedges that earned their keep were the three where
the pool backed the visitors. Nottingham Forest beat Manchester City 3–1,
Preston beat Southampton 1–0 and Charlton beat Sheffield United 2–1. Each time
the coupon kept the home side in. Cardiff beat Burnley 5–4 after trailing 1–2 at
half-time, and a hedge without the draw survived nine goals. Burnley beat the coupon
three times in October. This time it cost nothing.

Arsenal beat Hull 1–0 and Birmingham beat Swansea 2–1. Nearly the whole pool
had Arsenal, and it was still goalless at the break. A single only has to come
in, and it did. I won't pretend it looked comfortable.

**What I got wrong.** Three singles, all on the pool's pick, all beaten. Fulham
drew 1–1 with Newcastle and Middlesbrough drew 0–0 at Watford. West Ham were
the costliest. They lost 2–1 at Blackburn with West Ham backed alone. They'd
lost 2–3 at home to Southampton on 24 October and 1–3 at home to West Brom a
week ago, and that second defeat broke a hedge. Backing them alone a week later
was a bad read, and the pool agreeing doesn't make it a better one.

Fulham was a placement error more than bad luck. In the pool, only
Crewe–Wimbledon was more open than Fulham–Newcastle, and Fulham went in alone
while QPR, a far firmer favourite, got a hedge it didn't need.

Ten home wins, two draws and one away win. That's the most home wins and the
fewest away wins of any round this season. Stevenage's 1–0 at Oxford was the
only away win, and the hedge had it. The pool made the visitors favourites in
five matches and none of them won. 36 goals, the same as last week.

Note for the model. Singles are where the coupon leaks. They're 25 from 50 this
season and the hedges are 59 from 80. A single belongs on one of the clearest
favourites on the coupon. Don't play one on a match more open than a hedged
one unless the reason is written down before kickoff. Keeping the home side in
against a pool favourite on the road paid three times, so keep doing it. Crewe
carried the draw where the 31 October note said it should go, and didn't need
it. Nine draws in four rounds and none covered. No single went against the pool
for the second week running, so the 10 October rule still hasn't been tested.
