---
title: Post-round review
summary: 9 of 13. Level with the market on the twelve we priced, beaten on the one we didn't.
---
Nine of thirteen against an expected 8.12 covered — about half a standard
deviation above par. A decent afternoon the forecasting can take limited credit
for.

Mean RPS came in at 0.2308 against the market's 0.2250. The market wins by
0.0058, which sounds like nothing and mostly is — except the entire gap is one
match. On the twelve fixtures we actually held a price for, the forecast
finished fractionally ahead, 0.2344 to 0.2358. The thirteenth was Stockport.

**The round was lost on a match we never priced.** No closing line was logged
for Stockport v Wycombe on 25 August, so the forecast carried a hand estimate —
45% home, read off form and the venue — into the scored set. The close was 59%.
Stockport won 5–1. That single leg cost 0.09 of RPS against the market and
turned a level round into a losing one. The forecast wasn't wrong about the
winner; it was wrong to be in the sample without a price behind it.

**Note for the model.** A leg with no market price is a leg that shouldn't
score. Either a line gets logged before kickoff or the match comes off the
comparison. A guess dressed as a forecast is the exact thing this whole exercise
exists to prevent.

**Coventry is the one worth staring at.** The forecast backed the home side at
49%, the market at 51%, and the Veikkaus crowd at 38%. Hull won 0–1. The pool —
the least sophisticated number on the page — was the only one not badly long on
the home side. One match doesn't move a prior, but it's a data point filed under
"the crowd reads soft home favourites better than we do."

**Draws, still.** Three in thirteen against an expected 3.6 — the frequency was
fine this time. All three (Bournemouth, Watford, Cardiff) landed on outcomes the
coupon hadn't marked, Cardiff on a double with X squeezed out at 29%. Two rounds
running the excluded draw has been the miss. The volume isn't the problem any
more; the reflex to drop X to fit the system is.

**The structure carried it again.** Favourites went 6 of 13 — 46%, still short
of the 52–55% the model is meant to reach. The coupon scored 9 anyway because
Spurs, Blackburn and Bolton all lost as home favourites and all three were
doubled. Same shape as 22 August: the system does the work, and a 9/13 headline
isn't the same claim as good forecasting when only six top picks came in.
