title: Post-round review
summary: 9 of 13. Lost the mean to the market despite winning more matches. Draws remain the same hole.
---
Nine of thirteen against an expected 8.12 covered — about half a standard
deviation above par. A good-ish afternoon, not a vindicating one.

Mean RPS came in at 0.2308 against the market's 0.2250. The market wins by
0.0058, small enough to mean little on its own, but not nothing set against
last week's dead heat at the market's own number. Two rounds in and the
market hasn't lost yet.

**The forecast won more matchups and still lost the average.** Eight of
thirteen matches went to the forecast on RPS, five to the market. That's the
kind of split that should read as a good week. It doesn't, because the
market's five wins ran bigger — Blackburn by 0.084, Norwich by 0.058,
Stockport by 0.045 — while the forecast's eight wins were mostly worth a
couple of hundredths apiece. Winning the count and losing the mean is what
happens when the misses are more confident than the hits.

**Coventry and Stockport point in opposite directions, and neither settles
anything.** At Coventry the market backed the home side harder than the
forecast did — 55% against 49% — and lost outright to an away win; the
forecast's caution was the better call there by the widest margin in the
round. At Stockport the market backed the home side harder again — 52%
against 45% — and this time the favourite won 5–1, punishing the forecast's
flatter read by almost the same margin in the other direction. Read
together, this isn't "trust the market more" or "trust the market less."
It's that the forecast is running consistently flatter than the market on
strong favourites in both directions, and which way that costs is close to a
coin flip until there's more than two data points to look at.

**The structure carried the round, not the forecasting.** Favourite hit rate
was 6 of 13 — 46%, still under the 52–55% ceiling that's the actual ambition
here. The coupon scored 9 of 13 anyway, because three matches where the top
pick was wrong (Tottenham, Blackburn, Bolton) were doubled and got caught
regardless. Same shape as always: the system does real work, and a good
coupon score isn't the same claim as good forecasting when the numbers
underneath it don't say the same thing.

**Draws are still the same hole as last time.** Bournemouth, Watford, and
Cardiff all resolved X, none of the three was backed, and that's now two
rounds running.
