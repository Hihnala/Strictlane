---
title: Post-round review
summary: Beaten by the closing market across the round, not on one match. Stockport was the loudest leg, not the whole story.
---
A good afternoon for the coupon that the forecasting can take limited credit
for, and a round the closing market won.

**Correction, 10 September.** This review first said the round was lost on a
single match, Stockport v Wycombe, and that the forecast was otherwise level
with the market. That was measured against prices logged on the Tuesday before
the round plus one late snapshot. Against football-data's closing average, which
the site now uses for every match, it doesn't hold: Stockport's closing price
was well short of the figure quoted here, the leg cost far less than claimed,
and the market beat the forecast on the other twelve as well. The original text
is in the repository history.

**Where it was actually lost.** Blackburn v QPR and Norwich v Burnley. Both were
written on the Tuesday, four days out, as close matches with a home lean. By
kickoff the market had moved hard, towards QPR in one and towards Norwich in the
other, and both results went the way the money went: QPR won 2–1 at Ewood Park,
Norwich won 4–1. Both were doubled, so the coupon caught them. The probabilities
didn't. A forecast written four days before kickoff gives the market four days of
team news for free.

**Stockport, still.** The Stockport forecast was written with no price behind
it, a hand estimate read off form and the venue. The close had Stockport clearer
favourites than I did, they won 5–1, and it was one of the round's costliest
legs. The lesson survives the correction: a forecast gets written against a
logged price, or it doesn't get written.

**Coventry is the one worth staring at.** The forecast backed the home side, the
closing market backed them harder, and the Veikkaus crowd backed them least.
Hull won 0–1. The pool, the least sophisticated number on the page, was the only
one not long on the home side. It was also the forecast's best leg against the
close, which says more about the market's afternoon than about mine. One match
doesn't move a prior, but it's a data point filed under "the crowd reads soft
home favourites better than we do."

**Draws, still.** Three draws, at Bournemouth, Watford and Cardiff, and all three
landed on outcomes the coupon hadn't marked, Cardiff on a double with X squeezed
out. Two rounds running the excluded draw has been the miss. The volume isn't the
problem any more; the reflex to drop X to fit the system is.

**The structure carried it again.** Favourites came in less than half the time,
still short of where the model is meant to be. The coupon hit anyway because
Spurs, Blackburn and Bolton all lost as home favourites and all three were
doubled. Same shape as 22 August: the system does the work, and a good coupon
headline isn't the same claim as good forecasting.
