---
title: Post-round review
summary: 8 of 13. The forecast beat the market for the first time — while favourites had their worst week yet.
---
Eight of thirteen against an expected 8.86 covered — about half a standard
deviation below par, the mirror image of the round before it, just on the
other side of zero. A rougher afternoon, not a broken one.

Mean RPS came in at 0.2089 against the market's 0.2149. The forecast wins by
0.0060 — small, and on thirteen matches it proves about as much as it
disproves, but it's worth saying plainly: the market's run of not losing to
this site is over. Level on 22 August, a loss on 29 August, a win now.

**Draws did the damage again, for the third round running.** Five landed
against an expected 3.31 — not an extreme miss by itself — but four of the
five were on marks that had already excluded X: Liverpool 0-0 Fulham, Chelsea
2-2 Hull, Bournemouth 2-2 Brentford and Charlton 0-0 Portsmouth were all
singled or doubled onto the wrong two signs. Only Spurs 0-0 Everton was
covered, and that was luck — X happened to be one half of that double. Every
miss this week but one was a draw the coupon had already written off. Three
rounds in, this isn't bad luck any more; it's what an 8+0 system does when it
has to drop a sign somewhere and keeps dropping the same one.

**Favourites had their worst week of the season.** Four of thirteen came in —
31%, well under the 52–55% the model is meant to clear and the lowest yet.
The coupon still returned 8/13 because four of the nine upsets were still the
second sign on a double: Aston Villa 1-2 Nottingham Forest, Blackburn 3-1
Millwall and Watford 1-2 Stoke all backed both teams; Spurs' draw with
Everton was saved by the X half of a 1X. Crystal Palace 2-3 Ipswich wasn't so
lucky — marked 1X, so the away win backed here on affection alone (Ipswich is
a declared soft spot) went down as a clean miss. No asterisk for that one.

**The wins came from being less sure than the market, for once.** Chelsea's
71% was the round's biggest edge over the market's 76.6% when Hull held them
to 2-2, and Aston Villa's 39% did the same against the market's 43.1% when
Forest won away. Both times the forecast was already the more cautious number
and the caution paid. On 29 August the same flatness cost; here it earned.
Two results either way isn't a rule yet — it's a coin that's landed once each
way.
