---
title: Championship MW8
summary: Cardiff finally won, Charlton kept losing, and Norwich conceded four goals for the second week running.
---
**Long overdue.** Cardiff City 3–1 Charlton. Seven games without a win, a
record-equalling run for the club, ended with two goals from Jack Moylan
inside the first 23 minutes. Charlton arrived sixth in the table with only one
league defeat all season and left with two — Tyreece Campbell pulled one back
before half-time, but Moylan's second, in the 61st, put it beyond doubt. Three
weeks ago Charlton held the division's only unbeaten record. This is now three
defeats in five, and the drop from top to mid-table has been steep enough to
deserve tracking rather than shrugging off as a blip.

**A repeating problem.** Norwich City 3–4 Bolton. Second matchweek running
Norwich have lost by an identical 4–3 scoreline, having gone down by the same
margin at Middlesbrough the previous Saturday. Mohamed Touré's fifth-minute
opener was cancelled out twice before half-time, and Bolton substitute Atsuki
Ito settled it in the 72nd. Eight goals conceded in two matches against
bottom-half opposition is not bad luck twice over — it is a pattern, and
Norwich sit 17th because of it.

**Run ended.** Wrexham 2–1 Southampton. Kieffer Moore headed the hosts ahead
against the run of play in the 35th, James Ward-Prowse levelled five minutes
into the second half with his first goal since returning to the club, and
Wrexham won it in the 88th when Jacques Ekomié's cross was turned in off Keven
Schlotterbeck. Southampton had the better of the chances for long stretches
and still lost, which ended a five-match unbeaten run and is a more useful
sentence than any number of points earned since the deduction.

**Note for the model.** West Ham drew 2–2 at Millwall, their first dropped
points since a run that included a 6–0 win over Wrexham. Two winless matches
as favourites in August, four straight wins after — the early caution about
treating a newly relegated club as an automatic pick looks to have been about
form finding its level, not about the club itself being overrated. Worth
remembering the next time a parachute side starts slowly.
