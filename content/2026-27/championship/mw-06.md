---
title: Championship MW6
summary: A midweek round that ended two unbeaten runs, left two clubs still winless in six, and carried no forecast at all.
---
**Two runs, one night.** Birmingham and Swansea both arrived unbeaten in five
and both left beaten. Norwich took Birmingham 2–1 at Carrow Road; Southampton
put three past Swansea. Neither run had looked fragile going in, which is
usually how these end — not with a collapse, just with a Tuesday.

**Southampton, still climbing.** That 3–1 makes it five without defeat since the
opening day at Watford, and eleven points earned. They are on seven. The
four-point deduction will be paid off and forgotten by October, which is
roughly the opposite of what a sporting sanction is for — though that is the
EFL's problem to have, not Southampton's.

**Back to earth.** Charlton 0–0 QPR. Three weeks ago they held the division's
only perfect record; since then they have been beaten 4–0 at Stoke and failed
to score at home. A goalless draw is a sensible way to stop the bleeding and a
poor way to stay top.

**Still nothing.** Burnley drew at Wrexham, Cardiff drew with Stoke, and both
are six matches into the season without a win. Two results that suited nobody
who took part in them.

**Three that didn't happen.** The Carabao Cup third round took two of them —
Middlesbrough were at Crystal Palace, Millwall hosted Newcastle and Lincoln
were at Bournemouth on the same evening — so Middlesbrough–Millwall and Bristol
City–Lincoln simply move to 15 September, a week late and nobody much
inconvenienced. Portsmouth–Wolves is the awkward one: a fixture clash has sent
it to 20 October, six weeks adrift of the round it belongs to. Matchweek six
will not be a finished matchweek until late autumn, and whatever the table says
about those six clubs before then is a provisional reading.

**Note for the model.** Not one of the twelve carried a forecast, because the
coupon is a Saturday product and this was a Tuesday. Midweek rounds add
fixtures to the record and nothing whatever to the calibration. The gap widens
every time one comes around, and it is better named than quietly tolerated.
