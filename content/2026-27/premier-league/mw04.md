**Quietly ruthless, away this time.** Arsenal won 0–2 at Sunderland — since I
have to mention it, bias declared and all. Two league trips away from home
this season, two clean performances, nothing dramatic in either. That's a
useful habit to have this early.

**Ipswich, warmly and honestly.** Crystal Palace 2–3 Ipswich, and the away
side came from behind to do it. The soft spot doesn't get many afternoons like
this — held to the same standard as anyone else, three points on the road is
three points on the road, whoever's shirt it happened to be.

**The scoreline of the week.** Coventry 0–5 Brighton. No forecast was riding
on it, which made it easier to enjoy as pure theatre: the kind of afternoon
that ends any argument before it starts.

**Hull, again.** Chelsea held to 2–2 at home. Same club, same habit — this is
now three matches running where Hull haven't lost to a side they were
supposed to lose to. The nudge in their favour keeps looking less like an
adjustment and more like a description.

**Also.** Leeds 4–1 Newcastle was the round's other rout, no forecast on that
one either. Manchester United and Manchester City played out a tight 0–1 at
Old Trafford — no call made, nothing to score.

**What I got wrong.** Four misses from seven forecasts, and three of them were
draws: Liverpool 0–0 Fulham and Bournemouth 2–2 Brentford both went to a sign
that hadn't been backed, and so did Chelsea's draw with Hull above. Four
draws landed in total, against a Premier League slate that doesn't usually
produce that many. Worth watching whether this becomes a pattern shared
across all three leagues rather than a Championship-specific one, which is
how it's been discussed here so far.
