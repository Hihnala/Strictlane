---
title: Premier League MW2
summary: Chelsea win a seven-goal game on a quarter of the ball, Villa go two matches without a shot on target, and both our singles missed.
---
**Biggest surprise.** Chelsea 4–3 Brighton. Brighton arrived top of the table
having put four past Villa and four past Tromsø inside a week, and lost a
seven-goal game to a side that had 26% of the ball and completed 155 passes.
Alonso's Chelsea barely pressed at all — they sat off, waited, and scored four
times anyway. Ruthless on the counter and a defence that conceded three at
home. Both of those are true, and only one of them survives a harder fixture.

**Worst afternoon.** Aston Villa. Two matches, no goals, and the number that
actually indicts them: no shot on target in either. Buendía clipped Raya's
crossbar on twelve minutes and that was the whole of Villa's evening — an xG of
0.3 against opponents who managed 1.05 themselves. The last time Villa lost
their opening two without scoring was 2002.

**Arsenal, since I have to.** Saka finished from close range in the second half
and Arsenal were nowhere near the level of the Coventry night. Unchanged
eleven, long spells of possession, one goal, and a Tzolis booking that made the
half-time substitution compulsory rather than tactical. Winning badly is a
useful habit. It is not the same as being good, and saying so is the entire
point of writing any of this down.

**Ipswich.** Led at Old Trafford on 29 minutes, lost 5–2. The scoreline
flattered United, which changes nothing about the scoreline.

**What I got wrong.** Both singles, both in this league. Bournemouth–Everton at
46% home finished 1–1. Coventry–Hull at 49% home lost to the away side — and
that was the one match where the forecast deliberately sat under the market's
~54%. The disagreement was argued in advance. It was also wrong.
